### Overall Trend Analysis

IR as a category has shown a **clear upward trajectory**, climbing from 14 questions in 2022 to 15 in 2025, with 2023 being an anomalous dip likely due to GS-Paper restructuring that year. The thematic focus has decisively shifted from **pure geopolitics and territorial disputes** (dominant pre-2022) toward **institutional mechanics** — UPSC now tests whether a candidate knows the specific charter conditions, founding declarations, membership criteria, and summit outcomes of multilateral organisations rather than merely their existence. A critical structural change between 2022 and 2025 is the **rise of assertion-reason (Statement I / Statement II) format** in IR, which appeared heavily in 2025 (COP28, Paris Agreement Article 6, BIMSTEC, IBRD), demanding that candidates not only know facts but assess causal relationships between them. The **Organisations & Forums cluster dominates** with 21 of 48 questions, confirming that UPSC views IR primarily through the lens of institutional architecture rather than bilateral narratives.

**What UPSC is testing:**
- **Founding documents and declarations** (Dhaka Declaration for BIMSTEC, not Bangkok Declaration — a classic UPSC trap)

- **Binding vs. non-binding nature of declarations**: COP28 Climate & Health Declaration is non-binding — Statement II in 2025 question was a deliberate trap

**Question format pattern:**
Multi-statement questions dominate (3–4 statements, "how many are correct" format increasingly used in 2024–25). Direct factual questions ask: "Which organisation enacted X?" or "Which is correct about Organisation Y?" The assertion-reason format (Statement I / Statement II) specifically targets **causal misconceptions** — e.g., India not signing COP28 declaration doesn't mean the declaration is binding.

5. **IOMed (International Organization for Mediation)** — World's first intergovernmental org dedicated exclusively to mediation, newly established — classic UPSC "new institution" question

**Economic Survey / Budget angle:**

### Agreements & Treaties

**Questions tested (2022–2025):**
- UNCLOS (territorial sea 12 NM, EEZ 200 NM, innocent passage — 2022, all three statements correct)
- Polar Code (safety for ships in polar waters — 2022)
- G20 Common Framework (debt relief for LICs, Paris Club endorsement — 2022)
- Global Compact on Migration (binding/non-binding status, governance structure — 2023)
- Alma-Ata Declaration (healthcare), Talanoa Dialogue (climate), Under2 Coalition (child rights trap), Hague Convention (biological/chemical weapons trap) — 2024 match-the-pairs
- Paris Agreement Article 6 (carbon markets, cooperative approaches — 2024 and 2025)
- Hague Convention match-the-pairs — **classic trap**: Hague Convention covers child abduction and custody, NOT biological/chemical weapons (that's the Chemical Weapons Convention / Biological Weapons Convention)

**What UPSC is testing:**
- **Exact provisions**: UNCLOS Article specifying 12 NM territorial sea, 200 NM EEZ — not approximate knowledge
- **Binding vs. non-binding**: Global Compact on Migration is non-binding; Paris Agreement is binding but Article 6 mechanisms are not yet fully operationalised
- **Correct matching of declarations to subjects**: Alma-Ata = primary healthcare (not vaccines); Talanoa Dialogue = climate (Fiji-initiated); Under2 Coalition = sub-national climate action (not child rights — 2024 trap)
- **Specific clauses**: Paris Agreement Article 6 = carbon trading/cooperative mechanisms (not mitigation targets — that's Article 4)
- **India's participation**: Is India a signatory? What reservations did India make?

**Question format pattern:**
Match-the-pairs and multi-statement are equally common. The **"which pairs are correctly matched"** format is uniquely dangerous here because UPSC deliberately puts plausible-but-wrong pairings (Hague Convention = bio-chem weapons is the canonical example). Statement-based questions test whether students know **binding status** and **specific article numbers**.

**Current affairs linkage (2025–2026):**
1. **Paris Agreement Article 6 operationalisation (COP29, Baku 2024)** — Carbon credit market rules (Article 6.2 bilateral and 6.4 centralised mechanism) were finalised at COP29; highly testable in 2026 as UPSC asked this in both 2024 and 2025
2. **India-UK Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA)** — Signed/concluded in 2025; Economic Survey explicitly references this; UPSC may test which sectors are liberalised or India's first CEPA with a major economy
3. **India-EFTA TEPA** — USD 100 billion binding investment commitment; first trade deal with binding investment commitment clause; EFTA members (Switzerland, Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein) are testable
4. **UNCLOS Continental Shelf Claim** — India filed new continental shelf claim beyond 200 NM; CLCS recommendations are "final and binding" — directly from study material, testable in 2026
5. **Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework (GBF) implementation** — "30x30" target (protect 30% of land and sea by 2030); binding financial mechanism still under negotiation — parallels Paris Agreement Article 6 pattern UPSC loves

**Economic Survey / Budget angle:**
Budget 2025 references India-Israel Bilateral Investment Treaty signing and India-EFTA TEPA's USD 100 billion investment commitment as key FDI milestones — UPSC may test which of these are binding vs. aspirational commitments.

## Section 4: What UPSC Actually Wants From IR

UPSC's IR questions reveal a **three-layer cognitive architecture** that rewards analytical precision over encyclopaedic breadth.

The **first and deepest layer** is **institutional forensics** — UPSC does not ask "what is BIMSTEC" but rather "was BIMSTEC founded by the Dhaka Declaration or the Bangkok Declaration?" It does not ask "does India belong to BRICS" but rather "is the 16th BRICS Summit theme correctly stated?" This demands that Rahul study organisations the way a lawyer reads a statute: founding instrument, exact membership conditions, binding vs. non-binding outputs, and India's precise role (founding member, later joiner, observer, or non-member). The single most common trap is **confusing two similar-sounding agreements** (Alma-Ata vs. Alma Ata + healthcare vs. vaccines; Hague Convention vs. CWC/BWC).

The **second layer** is **causal-logical reasoning** — the Statement I / Statement II format tests whether a candidate understands *why* something is true, not just *that* it is true. India not signing the COP28 Climate & Health Declaration does NOT mean the declaration is binding — these are independent facts. UPSC is testing whether Rahul can break a causal chain that sounds intuitive but is logically flawed.

The **third layer** is **India's strategic positioning** — across bilateral, multilateral, and treaty questions, there is a consistent sub-theme: **what India joined, what India did not join, and why**. India's non-membership of CPTPP, its withdrawal from RCEP, its membership in MSP but import-dependence on critical minerals, its founding role in ISA and INSTC — these are not trivia but the architecture of India's foreign policy identity.

**Study prescription**: For every organisation or treaty, Rahul must know five things — founding year and instrument, India's precise membership status, one thing it IS and one thing it is NOT, and the most recent summit/update. The format will be statement-based; the trap will be one correct + one false statement.
